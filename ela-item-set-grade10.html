    Development Notes

    • Blueprint coverage. Key ideas and details (Items 2, 4, 5), craft and structure (Items 1, 3, 6), and integration of knowledge and ideas (Item 7). Depth of Knowledge ranges from Level 1 to Level 3, with three items at Level 3.
    • Why an informational text. This set pairs with the grade 5 literary set to show range across grade bands and genres: here, students track a cause-and-effect argument across a century and evaluate how well an author supports a claim.
    • Accuracy. Historical claims are limited to well-documented points (the 1921 English-only provision for public schools, the 1998 dictionary of Louisiana Creole, the language’s endangered status), and the article signals where scholars disagree or where numbers are uncertain. In production, every fact would go through a source-verification check.
    • Bias and sensitivity review. The origins of the language in slavery are stated plainly and without graphic detail. The article distinguishes Louisiana Creole from Cajun French and from standard French, presents disagreements among speakers neutrally, and centers elders and community members as experts. No item depends on prior knowledge of Louisiana.
    • Distractor design. Each distractor reflects a specific misreading: an overstated detail, a reversal of the author’s stance, a true detail that does not answer the question, or an unsupported prediction.
    • AI-assisted workflow. See the AI item-review case study for how AI drafts are prompted, audited, and revised before any item enters a set.

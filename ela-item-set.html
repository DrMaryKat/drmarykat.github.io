    Development Notes

    • Blueprint coverage. Key ideas and details (Items 2, 3, 4, 5, 8), craft and structure (Items 1, 6, 7), and vocabulary acquisition (Items 1, 7). Depth of Knowledge ranges from Level 1 to Level 3, with three items at Level 3.
    • Difficulty targets. Difficulty labels are author estimates prior to field testing, with target p-values of .70 to .85 (Easy), .50 to .70 (Medium), and .30 to .50 (Hard). Final classifications would be set from field-test statistics and distractor analysis.
    • Distractor design. Every distractor is plausible and tied to a specific misreading: a detail that appears in the text but does not answer the question, a partial understanding, or an overgeneralization. No "all of the above," no negative stems, and options are parallel in length and structure.
    • Bias and sensitivity review. The cultural setting is presented from the inside and without stereotype; family terms (Mon-mon, Chère, Bébé) are glossed with pronunciations, are understandable from context, and are not tested. Stovetop cooking happens with an adult present. No item requires background knowledge of candy making; every answer is supported by the passage.
    • Accessibility. Numbered paragraphs for reference, keyboard alternatives for both technology-enhanced items (move buttons for ordering; selectable text that works with Enter or Space), visible focus states, and live feedback announced to screen readers.
    • AI-assisted workflow. Drafts can be stress-tested with generative AI (for example, asking a model to argue for each distractor), but every key, rationale, and alignment decision in this set was written and verified by the author against the passage and the standards.

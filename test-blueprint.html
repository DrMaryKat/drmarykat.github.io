Part 3Item Specification: RL.5.3

The guidance item writers receive for one standard.

Standard
RL.5.3: Compare and contrast two or more characters, settings, or events in a story or drama, drawing on specific details in the text (for example, how characters interact).
Evidence statements
  • The student identifies similarities or differences between two characters, settings, or events using details from the text.
  • The student explains how a character's actions or feelings differ at two points in the story.
  • The student describes how two characters interact and uses details to support the description.
Item types and points
Multiple choice (1); two-part EBSR (2); hot text (1); short constructed response (2)
Depth of Knowledge
DOK 2 for single comparisons; DOK 3 when students must synthesize details across the text or explain a change
Stimulus requirements
A literary passage with at least two developed characters, or one character shown in at least two contrasting scenes or events
Sample stems
  • How is [character]'s approach in paragraphs [x] to [y] different from [his/her] approach in paragraphs [x] to [y]?
  • How are [character A] and [character B] alike?
  • Which detail best shows how [character A] and [character B] interact?
  • Part A: How does [character] change? Part B: Which detail best supports the answer to Part A?
Key and distractor rules
  • The key states a comparison supported by details from both sides of the comparison.
  • Distractors may: reverse the comparison; describe only one side; use an accurate detail from the wrong point in the story; or state a difference the text does not support.
  • Options name specific actions or traits, not vague labels such as "nice" or "different."
Exclusions
  • Comparisons across two different texts (assess with RL.5.9).
  • Items that only ask for a theme or a summary (assess with RL.5.2).
  • Items that require knowledge from outside the passage, or that ask students to judge a character's culture or family.
Accessibility
Paragraph references in every stem; plain syntax; options parallel in length; hot text segments of complete sentences; keyboard operation for all technology-enhanced formats
Exemplars
The Praline Pot, Items 2 and 8; the revised item in the AI item-review case study

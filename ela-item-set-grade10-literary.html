Grade 10 Literary Item Set: What the Recorder Heard

An original short story about a high school student who records her great-aunt speaking Kouri-Vini for a class project and learns what translation can and cannot carry. Eight standards-aligned items include selected response, an evidence-based two-part item, two technology-enhanced items, and an extended constructed response with a scoring rubric. It pairs thematically with the grade 10 informational set, Going and Coming. Switch to Item Specifications for keys, rationales, metadata, and scoring guidance.

    Development Notes

    • Blueprint coverage. Key ideas and details (Items 2, 3, 5, 8), craft and structure (Items 4, 6, 7), and vocabulary (Items 1, 6). Depth of Knowledge ranges from Level 1 to Level 3, with three items at Level 3.
    • Paired with the informational set. This story and Going and Coming share a topic, the loss and revival of Kouri-Vini, but no tested content overlaps. Together they could support a paired-text task (RL.9-10.9 or W.9-10.9) comparing how a story and an article treat the same subject.
    • Passage revision for level. The first draft measured about 990L because of its short dialogue lines. I lengthened and combined narrative sentences, kept the dialogue and the two one-line paragraphs that carry the story’s tension, and revised until the estimate reached the grades 9 to 10 band. The story’s qualitative complexity (extended metaphor, implied theme, a reframing ending) supports placement at grade 10.
    • Cultural accuracy and sensitivity. Family terms and Kouri-Vini phrases follow the author’s chapter-book glossary and are glossed, never tested. The story treats the language, the elder, and the mother’s partial loss of the language with respect and without stereotype. The translation app is a plot device, not a claim about any real product.
    • Distractor design. Distractors are built from specific misreadings: literal readings of figurative language, reversals of the narrator’s growth, details from the wrong part of the story, and overreadings of the ending.
    • Key balance. Selected-response keys are distributed B, D, A, C, C, D across the set.
